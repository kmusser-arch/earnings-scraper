# Earned Lessons

Every rule here cost something to learn. Each has a founding case. **Apply them; do not re-derive them.**

---

## A. POSITIONING — the variable that sets the sign

### A1. ★ Run-Into-Print is the strongest signal in the system
**Print-day move ÷ implied move. Correlation with reaction: −0.832 — stronger than the entire fundamental scorecard (+0.800).** One division. Compute it first, every time.

### A2. ★ Never lock Run-Into-Print from an intraday snapshot
Use the **official close**, two independent OHLC sources. If the print is same-day, mark it `PENDING`.
**Founding case:** SNDK 8/5/26 — a single search result reported +10.65% on the print day and the whole read was built on "PRE-PAID VETO, worse than ALAB," forecasting −12% to −20%. The verified close was **−5.40%**. The stock had *de-risked* into its print and closed **−6.81%** at 0.43x implied. WDC had the same error the same night (+1.31% snapshot vs a −5.36% close).

### A3. Positioning sets the required surprise, not the direction
**Founding case:** Kyle, 8/5/26 — *"amd yesterday, the earnings were fine but it sold off because there was extreme long positioning and unless something crazy happened it was probably not going to go up."* AMD Q1 and Q2 2026: same company, same crowded tape, opposite outcomes. Q1 carried an unmodeled 2027 DC AI figure (+17%); Q2 carried three qualitative heroes with no dollar anchor (−8%).

### A4. ★ The escape clause must name WHICH metric
At ≥7.0 crowding the gate demands a guide >+3% vs bogey. **Clearing it on the wrong line does not count.**
**Founding case:** CSCO Q4 FY26 — guide beat consensus by **+6.0% FY27 revenue, +9.5% FY27 EPS, +8.4% and +19.8% on Q1** and the stock fell **8.40%**. The market graded **gross margin**, for which no published consensus even existed. Every pre-earnings build must name the single graded line; where no consensus exists for it, that absence is itself a red flag.

### A5. The two-timeframe rule
When the 1-year and last-5-session windows disagree, **the short window governs** — it identifies the marginal holder.
**Founding case:** AXTI Q2 2026 — 1-year read "crowded"; the four sessions into the print were −21.7%. The short window was right: **+44%**.

### A6. Fade patterns are REGIME-CONDITIONAL
Before citing any base rate, ask whether it was established in the same chart/multiple regime being traded now.
**Founding case:** PLTR 8/3/26 — five consecutive beat-and-raises had been sold, median next-day −0.04%. But all five happened during active multiple compression (P/S 127x → 58x). By Q2 the compression had ended and it ran **+15%**. Kyle bought it against the base rate and was right.

### A7. Short interest is a weak standalone crowding proxy
Correlation −0.156. Convert arb, GC borrow and low days-to-cover all break it.
**Founding case:** CIFR's headline 16.71% of float was almost entirely delta hedge against $1.3B of 0.00% converts — 69–77M implied hedge shares against 56M reported short.

### A8. The sheet positioning score anchors components 1–2 but predicts nothing alone
Correlation +0.066. It is fixed 1–3 days early and cannot contain component 3.
**Founding case:** ANET and ALAB both scored exactly **7** on the same sheet. ANET **+12%**, ALAB **−2%**. The differentiators — ALAB had pre-paid its implied move, ANET had a call-only FY raise — are both things a sheet cannot carry.

### A9. Mechanical supply events need a slot
Lockup tranches, index rebalances, 10b5-1 windows.
**Founding case:** CBRS lost an extra **5.21%** on day two when **36.4M shares came off lockup at 6:00 am on 8/14** — contractual and published. Remaining ladder: 138M shares by late October, main expiry the earlier of 9 Nov 2026 or two trading days post-Q3.

### A10. ★ The sector bar-raiser — runs both directions, still unmodelled
**Negative:** Seagate printed nine days before WDC at 52.7% GM with a ~57.5% September target, ~200bps above WDC's guide. Cantor opened WDC's call with exactly that comparison; Wedbush named it as the reason. **WDC −13.03%.**
**Positive:** CoreWeave printed strong 8/11 AMC and NBIS was already **+8% in after-hours** before its own 8/12 release.
Three occurrences in two weeks (Intel→AMD, Seagate→WDC, CRWV→NBIS). **Highest-priority missing component.**

## B. DISCLOSURE QUALITY — what actually re-rates a stock

### B1. Unmodeled disclosures have no consensus denominator
Measure against the **current run rate**, not consensus. **>2x run-rate = model rebuild.**
**Founding case:** AMD Q1 2026 — "tens of billions of 2027 DC AI revenue" against a ~$23B annualised DC run rate. No street 2027 DC AI line existed, so there was no percentage to compute. Revenue printed **+0.03% vs bogey** and the stock rose **+17%**.

### B2. ★ Read $-anchored disclosures for CEILINGS, not just floors
**Founding case:** SNDK Q2 2026 disclosed **$93.9B of minimum contracted revenue, $91.1B pro-forma RPO, $16.5B of financial guarantees, >4-year weighted duration** — and closed **−6.81%**. Because the same disclosure carried pricing **floors AND CEILINGS**, ~80% NBM gross margin (dilutive to the 84.6% just printed), and a **cut to FY27 sellable bit growth** to fund NBM inventory. It secured the floor and capped the model in one breath.

### B3. The $-anchor is worth roughly six points of downside protection
**Founding case — a clean natural experiment.** SNDK and WDC, same sector, same 4:30 PM call slot, both de-risked ~−5.4% into their prints, both guided light versus their bogeys. SNDK disclosed $93.9B → **−6.81%**. WDC disclosed the phrase *"increasing visibility"* and one signed LTA → **−13.03%**.

### B4. Guidance withheld from the PR = the call is the event
Track as a **per-ticker structural habit**.
**Founding cases:** AXTI 2 for 2 (Q1 +17.6%, Q2 +44% on a call-only guide 70% above consensus). NBIS Q2 2026 — the PR carried no guidance, no ARR, no backlog, no customer names and no pricing; the letter said verbatim that guidance would come on the call. It opened +17% and closed **+34%**.

### B5. Deposits without a total contract value are a setup signal, not the trade
**Founding case:** AXTI's Lumentum LTA disclosed **$87M of deposits with no TCV, no volumes, no pricing** — the 8-K reaction stopped at +27%. The earnings call supplied the anchor and the other +44% came the next day.

### B6. ⚡ The Asymmetric Event Flag
A vague-but-quantitative forward claim **lacking a dollar anchor** fires it. Three gates: MAGNITUDE, HORIZON, FORCEFUL, plus unambiguous direction. Bidirectional. **The flag exists to change behaviour: stay for the call.**
**Founding case:** ORCL Q1 FY26 — Catz disclosing RPO "likely to exceed half-a-trillion."

### B7. Forward-demand-pull, Tier 1/2/3
CEO/CFO quotes that pull demand forward. Kyle's stated #1 lesson, from missing AMD's 5/5/26 +15–20% rally.

### B8. The Disbelieved Re-Rate — three gates, needs all three
**NAMED customers · $-anchored BACKLOG · FY+1 revenue BOOKED.**
- SNDK Q2 2026: 2 of 3 (customers withheld) → narrative +0.5
- CRWV Q2 2026: 0 of 3 ($25B of new commitments completely unattributed) → rose on margin proof instead
- WDC Q4 FY26: 0 of 3 → narrative −1.5

### B9. The SCA Catalyst Override
Overrides a peak-cycle fade: **≥5-year take-or-pay · $100B+ cumulative backlog · ≥10% cash deposits · floor pricing.**
**Founding case:** MU Q3 2026, **+17%** despite missing a stretched buyside bogey.

## C. MANAGEMENT ACTIONS — what they do beats what they say

### C1. ★ ACTION cap-the-model
Founder-CEOs and mega-cap managements cap the model through **actions** while sounding bullish: removed roadmap language, delayed timelines, capex with no dollar anchor, **a withdrawn metric**, a volunteered growth ceiling. **Actions beat words. Diff the shareholder deck.**
**Founding cases:**
- TSLA Q2 2026: **−18–20%** despite Musk saying he had "never been more optimistic."
- APP Q2 2026: volunteered a first-ever *"compound at roughly 30% annually"* target **on a miss quarter** while printing 53% YoY. No analyst challenged it.
- CSCO Q4 FY26: **discontinued the FY27 AI order target** — the escalating number ($5B → ~$9B → $9.3B delivered) that drove the entire FY26 re-rate — replaced with "meaningfully higher."

### C2. The anti-cap: a roadmap raise with capex reaffirmed
**Founding case:** AXTI raised its InP capacity roadmap from "double" to "more than triple" while explicitly reaffirming capex — *"there is actually not a lot of additional CapEx spend here."* In a capex-punishment tape that was worth a premium.

### C3. Capex polarity is CONDITIONAL, not fixed
**Capex is not the variable — capex WITHOUT margin proof is.**
**Founding case:** CRWV raised capex in May 2026 and fell 10%; raised it again by **$4B** in August and rose **19.28%**. The difference was that August's raise arrived bundled with a revenue raise **and** a margin beat.

### C4. A reaffirm is a −1 for scale-economics stories, not a 0
**Founding case:** CRWV Q1 2026 reaffirmed FY guidance and fell 10%. Q2 raised it and rose 19.28%.

## D. GRADING TRAPS

### D1. ★ The expected column is as error-prone as the actual column
**Founding case:** an AAPL record stored Greater China consensus at **$21.5B** against a real street figure of **$19.0B**, and consequently graded a **7.9% BEAT as the print's biggest bear vector.** Kyle's instinct that the numbers looked off was right, and the error was on the expectations side. Re-verify consensus and bogeys, not just reported results.

### D2. ★ A company-defined non-GAAP metric can mask a GAAP miss
**Founding case:** CBRS Q2 2026 — GAAP revenue **$180.11M missed consensus by 7.0%** while company-defined "core" revenue **$209.87M beat by 9.9%**. Zacks published a fake **"+8.09% revenue surprise"** by comparing its GAAP estimate to the core actual. **The tape traded GAAP.** Always establish which basis consensus is on before grading.

### D3. GAAP/non-GAAP headline traps
**Founding case:** WDC Q4 FY26 GAAP EPS **$8.21** against a **$3.29** street estimate — a **$2,050M mark-to-market gain** on a retained stake. Comparable was non-GAAP **$3.56**. A headline algo sees a 150% beat that does not exist.

### D4. Definitional mismatches — flag, do not grade
**Founding case:** a PLTR "cash & equivalents" consensus of $3.25B against a company managing cash across cash + marketable securities ($9.2B combined). Grading it literally produces a false miss.

### D5. ★ Do not infer a unit or a legend from numeric plausibility
**Founding case:** an AMD sheet column reading "140%" was taken as front-week implied vol; it was **YTD move**. It produced a **false confirmation** because AMD's real ATM IV happened to be 137.3%. Related: `revUnit` is null on 7 of 76 records — refuse to grade rather than infer.

### D6. Units mismatches — suppress beyond ±300%
Computed deltas have hit +103,882% and −99.9% from mixed $M/$B or GAAP/non-GAAP bases. Suppress and set `pctFlag`.

### D7. Sign flips and wrong-day reactions
Always confirm: is this the print-day move, the after-hours settle, or the **next-day close**? Three are different numbers and only the next-day close belongs in `pctChangeNextDay`.
**Founding cases:** a PLTR record stored −1.37% when that was the print-day **+1.36% with a flipped sign** (true next-day: −6.93%). And four reaction figures supplied from memory in one week: NBIS "+31%" was the 2-day cumulative (print-day close **+34.14%**); CRWV "+18%" was an intraday tick (close **+19.28%**); CBRS "−7%" was the **GAAP revenue miss**, not a price move (close **−11.85%**); CSCO "−2%" was the *second* session (reaction **−8.40%**).

### D8. When sources conflict, store both
**Founding cases:** SNDK's FQ1 consensus had **four values** ($10.37B / $10.4B / $10.82B / $11.16B) and the **sign of the guide delta flipped** depending on which you picked. ZS and SNOW both carry unresolved reaction conflicts and are excluded from the overnight framework's case logic for that reason.

## E. TRADE MANAGEMENT

### E1. Guidance hierarchy — the scalp rule
A weak forward guide means **scalp only, never hold**, even on a good quarter. A weak-but-not-EVENT guide is also not a short. **Positioning decides which side pays.**
**Founding case:** the NOW vs GOOGL contrast, 7/22/26.

### E2. Chart-confluence override
A single-name **plus sector-ETF** daily setup overrides a negative fundamental base rate. Sector confluence is a legitimate second leg — it removes the "this is idiosyncratic" doubt.
**Founding case:** PLTR + IGV, 8/3/26.

### E3. Hold duration must inherit the entry thesis
If the conviction source is a multi-day structural setup, the natural hold is multi-day.
**Founding case:** PLTR 8/3/26 — Kyle entered and sized correctly on a swing thesis, then exited on an intraday clock. The overnight was the largest remaining increment: PR +6–8%, call settle **+14.97%**, next premarket **+17.3%**.

### E4. ★ The positive-gamma pullback — now 2 for 2
When net GEX is positive, dealers sell rallies and buy dips, so **the first post-catalyst pullback is manufactured — the highest-quality add, not a failure signal.** Corollary: positive gamma also **caps** the move.
**Founding cases:** PLTR 8/3/26 (net GEX +$175M; call wall $150 held $23.4M of gamma, which is why premarket chopped $144–147 instead of extending). CRWV 8/12/26 — gapped +20.35% to $108.70, sold to $105.44, reversed to $111.83. Kyle traded the pullback and closed at the highs.

### E5. Overnight holds pay only when after-hours could not finish pricing the print
Direction-agnostic. Four conditions, two vetoes. **Disqualifiers:** PR-legible in one line · already pre-paid the implied move · AH settle ≥1.5x implied · **whipsaw ratio >3x** (`(AH high − AH low) ÷ |AH net move|`).
**Founding cases:** AXTI +22.7pts of overnight upside; SPCX −4.5pts of overnight downside. ALAB failed at an **8.1x whipsaw** — a full round trip with no price you were comfortable carrying.

### E6. Peak-cycle buyside-inline fade
At stretched cycle positioning, **inline against a stretched bogey is a sell**; a bogey miss is a max short; only a nuke-crush (>110% of bogey) saves it.
**Founding case:** MU Q3 FY26, 6/24/26.

### E7. PR-vs-CALL score divergence is a first-class metric
Score the PR, re-score after the call, **log the delta.** It has predicted direction better than the PR score alone.
**Founding cases in one week:** SPCX **−1.65** (fell 12%), ANET **+1.55** (rose 12%), AMD +0.40, ALAB −0.55.

## F. PROCESS FAILURES — the meta-lessons

### F1. ★ "It rendered" is not "it works" — and this has happened twice
1. **12 records** had a missing `actuals.keyKPIs` array. The tab rendered blank rows with default INLINE verdicts and returned a healthy character count. The render test passed.
2. **Three cards** rendered in the grid and **threw on click**. The harness called `renderTab()` directly, never touching `cardHtml()` or `openModal()`, and reported "0 failures."

**Root cause of #2:** `status: 'pre-earnings'` lowercase made `isPre` false, and because **`null >= 0` is TRUE in JavaScript** the header called `.toFixed()` on null. **Assert the user-visible outcome, not the absence of an exception at an inner function.**

### F2. The scorecard is the first output; bookkeeping comes after
**Founding case:** Kyle, 7/22/26 GOOGL print. On a PR paste, zero tool calls before the read.

### F3. Format discipline
Kyle, emphatically: *"the format needs to be the same as pltr yesterday, amzn, meta, googl, etc. **stop changing the format.**"* Asymmetric flag at the top if it fires, then hero KPIs with street/bogey/actual, then the four categories.

### F4. Verify, do not assert
Claimed cards were "being written to the library" when they were not. **Check, then report.**

### F5. Never fabricate a category score or a trade summary
A fabricated score propagates into `beatMagnitude` and `branchAccuracyLog` and corrupts the model's own cross-sectional statistics. **A blank is strictly better.**

## G. WHERE THE MODEL CURRENTLY STANDS

**Correlations:** fundamental score vs reaction **+0.800** · positioning-adjusted **+0.865** · positioning alone **−0.645**.

**Branch accuracy, forward-written (8/5/26):** direction 3 of 3 · trigger identification 3 of 3 · **magnitude inside range 0 of 3.**
**Backfilled (8/10–14):** direction 3 of 4 · magnitude 0 of 4.

**Read that honestly: trigger logic works, magnitude calibration does not.** Both de-risked cases were under-sized and both crowded cases were under-sized in the other direction. The cells identify direction well and consistently understate magnitude at both extremes.

**Kyle traded 3 of 4 that week and went 3 for 3 on direction** — including CSCO and CBRS, both of which *raised guidance*. He did not trade NBIS, which was the largest move at +34.14% and sat in the same de-risked cell as CRWV, which he did trade and win. **The gap is coverage, not skill.**
